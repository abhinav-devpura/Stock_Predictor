Behind the build

How it was built

This started as a naive Reddit sentiment bot for a single stock. It ended as a cross-sectional quant pipeline with two edges that survived a rigorous validation gate — and five factor families that didn't. The rejections are the point.

  1. 0
    Where it started

    A single-stock sentiment bot

    v0 (predict_stock.py) scraped Reddit and scored headlines with TextBlob to predict one ticker. It ran — but had no way to tell whether the signal beat chance. The honest verdict was not valuable, and the question it raised — how would I even know if this works? — reframed the entire project.

  2. 1
    The foundation

    Build the referee before the players

    Before adding a single factor, I built an event-study validation gate: rank the universe, compare the top vs. bottom bucket's forward returns (Welch t-test), and require the effect to hold in both halves of the sample with the sign fixed a priori. Leakage was designed out — point-in-time as-of alignment on filing dates, a forward-return embargo, and non-overlapping sampling. The harness is self-tested on synthetic data (8/8) so I trust its verdicts.

  3. 2
    The graveyard

    Five factor families, zero survivors

    Calendar effects, momentum, post-earnings drift (PEAD), short-term reversal, and insider Form 4 buying — one by one, each was tested and rejected. The pattern was almost universal: strong in the early sample, faded or reversed in the late sample. Five unrelated signals decaying at the same split isn't coincidence — it was pointing at a regime shift, not alpha. The pattern became the finding.

  4. 3
    The turning point

    Was it real decay, or just too little data?

    I expanded the universe 3.4× (161 → ~565 names). If the fades were a power problem, t-stats should grow — and they did. But raw momentum and reversal still flipped out of sample even with the extra power, confirming genuine regime dependence rather than noise. Crucially, the expansion also pushed two different signals across the line for the first time.

  5. 4
    The trap I avoided

    The beta mirage

    An early top-decile portfolio looked like it minted money. It wasn't alpha — it was market beta in disguise. I re-measured every edge as a beta-neutral long-short (long the top decile, short the bottom), which cancels the market out and leaves only the true spread. That decomposition is the single most important guardrail in the project.

  6. ✓
    Edge #1

    Value — the first real signal

    Sector-neutral book-to-market is the only factor ever to pass the strict gate (both halves individually significant). Its beta-neutral long-short earns +8.9%/yr net of costs (Sharpe 0.75) and — unlike everything before it — strengthens late instead of fading. Honest caveat, documented prominently: value is the factor most inflated by my universe's survivorship bias, so I treat the magnitude with heavy skepticism.

  7. ✓
    Edge #2

    Text-change — "Lazy Prices"

    The idea: firms that heavily rewrite their 10-K/10-Q filings tend to underperform. On 40 tickers it failed — but that was underpowered, so I scaled to 150 and it passed (t=+2.45): section-level filing similarity, beta-neutral long-short +13.9%/yr net, Sharpe 0.85, stronger late. Here survivorship works in my favor — the short side is big-rewriters, and the ones that went bankrupt delisted, so the premium is understated.

  8. 5
    Today

    The screener & dashboard

    The two edges are genuinely uncorrelated (−0.13), so I combined them into one cross-sectional score — carefully weighting walk-forward (using only past data) to avoid the look-ahead trap that a naive in-sample fit would fall into. The result is the ranked board on the Dashboard.

Arriving at a mostly-null result — without shipping a market-beta portfolio dressed up as alpha — was the real accomplishment. The value delivered is the rigorous, reusable pipeline and the discipline that caught every trap along the way.

What this project demonstrates

  • Statistical rigor. An event-study gate, stability across time splits, non-overlapping sampling, and walk-forward weighting — applied uniformly and self-tested.
  • Leakage discipline. Point-in-time as-of alignment on SEC filing dates and a forward-return embargo, so no future information leaks into a signal.
  • Data engineering. A pipeline over SEC EDGAR XBRL fundamentals and full-text 10-K/10-Q filings, with rate-limited concurrent fetching and caching.
  • Intellectual honesty. Rejecting factors that didn't hold up, catching a beta mirage, and documenting survivorship bias in both directions rather than choosing the flattering read.
